# When does a recurring community composition warrant a name?

Astra’s discussion contribution for grid. The working definitions come from the full `plexichat.txt`. These are hypotheses for review before protocol registration.

## The type, the occurrence, and the sample

**Candidate plexitype.** A repeatable combination of microbial populations at a stated taxonomic resolution and ecological scale. Define the features and assignment rule on discovery data, then show where that rule works on independent observations. Record how much extra information members carry about each other after accounting for the environment. A site can contain overlapping groups and a sample can receive an uncertain assignment. A smooth response along a salinity gradient is an ecologically interpretable outcome that may make class boundaries unnecessary.

**Plexon.** Reserve this instance term for cases in which investigators can delimit an occurrence independently of the taxa used to recognize the type. Examples include one imaged particle or a tracked water parcel with measured boundaries. A bottle at a fixed station can sample several particles or successive parcels. With only bulk collections, report detections by station and date. Distinguish time between first and last detection of the *type*, duration of an individually tracked *occurrence*, and the unsampled interval during which apparent disappearance could reflect poor detection. Report observed habitats; biological host range requires evidence about hosts.

An independent putative exchange gives a recurring group a mechanism worth testing. Keep distinct evidence fields for reproducible membership, predicted exchange, and demonstrated dependence. Shared transcriptional responses and complementary gene inventories leave the donor, environmental supply, and fitness effect unresolved. The group discussion has yet to settle which evidence justifies removing “candidate.”

## What the primary studies establish

- **Recurrent group** is already a plankton-ecology term. Venrick used recurrent-group analysis to identify North Pacific diatom associations across epipelagic environments. A new label for ordinary co-occurrence would add little. Read the original abstract only. [Venrick, Recurrent Groups of Diatom Species in the North Pacific](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28973808/)
- **Seasonal recurrence** has an established benchmark. Fuhrman and colleagues measured monthly bacterioplankton fingerprints and oceanographic variables. Profiles at their coastal site returned toward similar compositions at annual lags, and environmental predictors explained variation in the community index. A calendar classifier would therefore reproduce a known result. Read main-text methods, results and Fig. 1. [Fuhrman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0602399103)
- **Detection depends on effort.** A single deeply sequenced English Channel sample recovered 95.4% of the pooled OTUs detected on 72 shallow survey dates. The denominator is the pooled shallow-survey OTU inventory. The observation at one date cannot establish continuous residence of each lineage. Read main-text methods and results. [Caporaso et al.](https://academic.oup.com/ismej/article/6/6/1089/7590018)
- **Community types compete with gradients.** Koren and colleagues found continuous variation in the abundance of dominant gut taxa and showed that apparent clustering depended on the cohort and analytical choices. This is a methodological precedent from another habitat. Fit a continuous alternative to the aquatic data and let it win. Read main-text results and Figs. 7–9. [Koren et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002863)
- **Composition and ancestry vary at different resolutions.** Lake Mendota’s strain analysis required median coverage above 10× on at least 30 dates spanning at least 10 years. This left 263 species eligible for SNV-profile analysis. Fig. 3 reports 39 of those 263 with both long-term and seasonal strain patterns. Freeze taxonomic resolution and report the callable subset before making a continuity claim. Read the published main text, Fig. 3 and SNV methods. [Rohwer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-024-01888-3)
- **A community average can describe few individual patches.** The taxon rank abundances on individual model chitin particles were poorly predicted by the average across particles, even with a shared source of seawater. A field study at Ocean Station Papa also compared individually isolated particles with composite material and found composition varied with particle characteristics. These studies give a physically observable meaning to “instance” and show why bulk recurrence alone cannot establish one. Read main-text results and sampling methods in both. [Model particle study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9335236/) · [Field particle study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811735/)

## Two tests that could change the terminology

**1. Do designated members predict an unseen member in another year?** Use one freshwater or marine site with repeated biological collections and overlapping seasons across years. Split by whole year or independent hydrographic event before discovering groups. Keep technical replicates, DNA/RNA pairs and samples from the same event together. In training only, select candidate members, freeze a subset as predictors and reserve the remaining members as outcomes. On held-out dates, compare detection probabilities from a model of season, water properties, sampling fraction, library depth and batch with predictions that additionally use the designated members. Match comparison groups on prevalence, seasonal niche and detectability. Include a continuous latent environmental gradient inferred only from background taxa, excluding candidate members. Use a count or detection model that accounts for library size and censored detections. Filtering or fitting a shared ordination across the train/test boundary would leak information.

Score the difference in held-out log predictive density per eligible biological collection. Average within events before aggregating over independently held-out events. Estimate uncertainty at that level. Preregister a practically meaningful gain and require replication across held-out years. A sufficiently precise gain below that threshold counts against the extra predictive claim; overlap with the threshold or confounding by batch makes the result inconclusive. Report all eligible and scored events, dates and taxa, missed detections and exclusions. A gain supports conditional predictability; unmeasured resource pulses remain a possible explanation.

**2. Does the inferred combination coexist on the proposed unit?** For a candidate with paired bulk and independent patch-resolved samples, freeze membership using bulk discovery data. Compare observed within-patch joint detections to an occupancy model that preserves taxon prevalence, patch size or type, collection event and sequencing effort. Use a separately held-out event. If adequately detected members consistently occupy different patches despite bulk recurrence, retire the single-patch interpretation at that scale. Without individual-patch observations, record “instance unresolved.” Independent snapshots give occupancy. Estimating duration requires repeated observations of the same unit or an explicit censored-duration design.

### A hydrographic occurrence has an observational precedent

Ottesen et al. sampled a drifting water mass off northern California and evaluated horizontal coherence using current velocity data, while recording strong vertical gradients along part of the track. Their heterotrophic populations showed synchronous transcriptional variation that the authors interpreted as consistent with responses to shared environmental variation. This is a useful positive control for following an occurrence and a caution for assigning direction to its proposed edges. A future eddy study should measure parcel identity independently, record tracking interruptions and distinguish the duration of successful tracking from the lifetime of the community. Read main-text Results and Fig. 1. [Ottesen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1222099110)

The number of distinct station labels measures metadata overlap, not the number of independent temporal replicates.

### Is the recurring object a group of taxa or a class of samples?

The choice changes the falsifier. A **group of taxa** can recur inside many different community states, and groups may overlap. A **class of samples** asserts a reproducible partition or mixture component in the full community. Conditional prediction of one taxon from its partners addresses the former. To justify naming a discrete state, compare a model with class membership to a flexible continuous model using *exactly the same observed taxa and environmental information*. Otherwise the class model could win merely because it saw more measurements. Distinct groups of taxa need not induce empty spaces between samples in an ordination. My preference is to name the recurring group, record an occurrence score with uncertainty, and test discreteness as a further prediction only when the manuscript actually claims it.

Conditional prediction has a direct methodological precedent. Wilkinson et al. predicted held-out frog occurrences using other species as observed predictors. This demonstrates a benefit obtainable without identifying a causal edge. Simulations by Zurell et al. also show that residual correlations depend on species prevalence even for a fixed interaction strength. Read the original main-text prediction example and the simulation paper’s accepted manuscript, methods and results. [Wilkinson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13518) · [Zurell et al.](https://www.dora.lib4ri.ch/wsl/islandora/object/wsl%3A18759/datastream/PDF2/Zurell-2018-Do_joint_species_distribution_models-%28accepted_version%29.pdf)
